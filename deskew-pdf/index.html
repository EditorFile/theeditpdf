When is deskewing useful?

Scanners and phone-camera scans almost always come out a degree or two off level, which makes documents look unprofessional and hurts OCR accuracy. Straightening first makes contracts, receipts, book scans and archived files easier to read, print and search — and a level page OCRs far more accurately than a tilted one.

Frequently asked questions

How does deskewing work here?

The tool measures each page's skew from its text baselines, then rotates the whole page content — text, images and lines together — by the opposite angle. The text stays real, selectable text; the page is not turned into a picture.

Will my PDF still be searchable after deskewing?

Yes, for pages with a text layer. Because the text itself is rotated rather than rasterised, you can still select, copy and search it afterwards.

What about pure image scans with no text?

Pages with no text layer cannot be measured from text. For those pages the tool offers an explicit raster option: the page is rendered as an image, rotated, and placed back — those pages are labelled as rasterised in the results, and their text is not searchable.

How much skew can it fix?

Up to 10° in either direction, which covers typical scanner skew. Pages straighter than 0.1° are left untouched, and pages skewed beyond 10° are flagged so you can check the scan.

Does it change my page size?

Slightly: a rotated page needs a larger box to fit, so straightened pages are a little wider and taller than the originals. Content is never cropped.

Is my PDF uploaded anywhere?

No. Deskewing runs entirely in your browser with pdf.js and pdf-lib. Your file never leaves your device.
