When is Bates numbering useful?

Bates stamps are the standard way to identify pages in litigation discovery, due-diligence data rooms, audits and insurance claims: every page gets a unique, citable number, so a reference like ABC-000123 points to exactly one page in exactly one file. Numbering a whole production in one sequence keeps exhibits consistent across hundreds of files.

Frequently asked questions

What is Bates numbering?

Bates numbering stamps a unique sequential number on every page of a legal document — for example ABC-000123 — so pages can be cited and tracked during discovery and litigation.

Can I number several files in one sequence?

Yes. Turn on 'Continue numbering across files' and the second file picks up where the first left off, so no number is used twice.

How are the numbers formatted?

You set the prefix (for example ABC-), the start number and the position on the page. Numbers are zero-padded automatically — 000123 — and the padding grows if the sequence outgrows it.

Will the stamp cover my document text?

Stamps sit in the page margin (36 points from the edge by default), so they should not cover body text. If a page has edge-to-edge content, pick the corner with the most empty space.

Can I remove Bates numbers later?

Stamps are burned into the page as text. They cannot be removed by this tool — keep a copy of the original unstamped file.

Is my document uploaded anywhere?

No. Stamping runs entirely in your browser with pdf-lib. Your files never leave your device — important for confidential legal documents.
